      Why I made this

      My family takes the same picture in the same spot in Yosemite Valley every autumn. Every year we'd stand there trying to remember how we managed it the year before. Where the camera went, who stood where, which way to aim. We'd get close. Close was never the same as right.

      What bugged me is that we're all walking around with a very good camera and a pile of sensors in one pocket, and none of that hardware was any help with the one job I actually wanted done. So I started building something that would put the sensors to work.

      The idea grew from there. The same corner of the garden every spring. The same first day of school picture, year after year.

      Tools and new technology have always fired up my creative side. Once the vision really started to come together in a working app, this became the way I wanted to share the idea with others.

      Step 01

      Start with any photo

      Anything on your device, or a scan of an old print. You can also use Photos near me to pull up shots that were taken close to where you're standing. Whichever you pick becomes the reference for the spot, and everything else works back from there.

      Step 02

      Plan the shot

      The app pulls the strong edges out of the old photo and puts them on your screen as focus lines. Getting back to the spot becomes a matter of walking until the lines land on the real thing, one at a time, instead of guessing from memory.

      It tells you how close you are the whole way in, and says so when you're standing in the right place.

      Step 03

      Line it up

      The app reads the old photo against what your camera is seeing and tells you which way to move. Move your feet, not just the phone, and watch where you're stepping. Once you're there the app holds both frames steady against each other so the fine detail lands where it should.

      Step 04

      Adjust and export

      Nudge, rotate or scale either photo until the two sit exactly how you want, then pick how the pair goes out. Side by side, stacked, one fading into the other, or as a wipe. Save the finished picture to your library or send it straight to someone.

      Nothing you make gets a watermark, and nothing ever will.

      It all happens on the phone

      Everything happens on the phone itself, and I never see any of it. Your photos don't hit a cloud, an edge server or a data lake. Nothing you make gets used to train an AI model.

      • No account. Nothing to sign up for and nothing to log into.
      • No servers. Every photo and every alignment stays on the device.
      • Never used for AI training. Not yours, not anybody's.
      • Location stays put. Used to get you back to the spot, and nowhere else.

      This is a tool, not a big tech company. I built it for myself, as a dad, a tinkerer, an explorer, a tech enthusiast and an amateur photographer. If you're fascinated by change, by history, or you love watching your kids grow up, this app is for you. Rob
