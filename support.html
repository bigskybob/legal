Reporting a problem

The more of this you include, the faster I can find it:

  • Your iPhone model and iOS version (Settings › General › About).
  • The app version. It's at the bottom of the app's own Settings screen.
  • What you were doing, and what happened instead of what you expected.
  • What kind of photo you started from: something you took recently, an old scan, or a picture from an archive.
  • A screenshot or a screen recording if you have one. Trim out anything you'd rather I didn't see; I only need the part that went wrong.

The usual questions

What do I need to run it?

An iPhone running iOS 17 or later. It's free at launch. An Android version and a companion iPad app are both on the way; sign up on the home page to hear when they land.

Is it really free?

Yes. The part you'll use most stays free: no ads, no watermark, and no cap on how many places you keep going back to. Further down the road there'll be a one-time unlock for some extra things I'm building, like timelapse and video export. Buy once, not a subscription, and nothing that works today gets moved behind it.

Where do my photos go?

Nowhere. Everything happens on the phone. There's no account, no server, and nothing you make is used to train an AI model. The privacy policy has the whole picture, and it's short.

What are the permissions for?
  • Camera runs the viewfinder and takes the new photo.
  • Photos lets you pick a reference photo, save a finished one, and, if you use Photos near me, find shots taken close to where you're standing.
  • Location (only while using the app) records where a photo was taken so the app can guide you back to the spot.
  • Motion matches your phone's tilt and rotation to the original photo.

You can change any of them later in iOS Settings, under the app's name. The feature that needs the permission stops working and the rest carries on.

How do I delete my data?

Delete a series in the app to remove its photos, or delete the app to remove everything. There's nothing on my side to delete, because nothing ever came to me. If you signed up for the email list, the unsubscribe link in any email takes you off it, or write to me and I'll remove you from the list entirely.

It won't line up. What am I doing wrong?

Probably nothing. Some scenes are much harder than others, and the honest answer is that patience does most of the work. A few things that help:

  • Move your feet, not just the phone. Most of the mismatch is where you're standing.
  • Line up one edge at a time. Get one long line to sit right, then work on the next.
  • Scenes with strong straight lines, like buildings and fences, are easier than trees and water.
  • Low light and featureless scenes are harder. When the phone can't track its position well, the app drops to a simpler mode that guides on the picture alone.
  • Watch where you're stepping. Look up often.

If a scene keeps beating you, tell me which one. That's exactly what I want to know.

Can I use an old photo from a library or the internet?

If you have the right to, yes. A photo you didn't take may belong to a family member, a library, an archive, or a photographer, and it's on you to check. In the United States a photo published before 1931 is very likely public domain by age, though an archive can set terms on its own scans. Credit the source either way. The terms say the same thing.

How does the beta work?

The beta runs through TestFlight, Apple's free app for trying things before they ship. When a wave opens I email you a link; TestFlight installs the app beside your normal apps and updates it when a new build goes out. To stop testing, delete the app, or open TestFlight and tap Stop Testing. Beta builds expire after 90 days on their own.

How do I get off the email list?

Every email has an unsubscribe link at the bottom. If you'd rather I delete you from the list entirely, write to me and I will.

Is there an Android or iPad version?

Both are on the way. Sign up on the home page and you'll hear the day each one lands.
